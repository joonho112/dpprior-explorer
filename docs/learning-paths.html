Learning paths

Three routes through the pool, with the settings to use and what to notice

A 25-minute beginner route, a 45-minute researcher route and a 90-minute course route through the DPprior Explorer widgets, each step with its link, time, setting and the number to notice, plus notes for instructors.

Each step opens a page (or a standalone widget with the setting already in its address). The times are for a first pass; every widget has more to try. The same routes are in the catalog as audience filters: beginners, researchers, a course.

Beginners: 25 minutes

From “why not a fixed number of clusters?” to the prior that says more than it seems to.

Step Widget Minutes Do Notice
1 Why nonparametric Bayes? (P-00) 4 K = 2, N = 100, then K = infinity, N = 500 Only the infinite model keeps opening new cells as N grows.
2 Stick-breaking (P-05) 5 alpha 0.5, 2, 10 Pr(w_1 > .5) = .5^alpha: .707 at alpha = 0.5, .25 at 2, below .001 at 10.
3 Chinese restaurant process (P-07) 6 alpha = 1: Seat 10, then Seat 100 Tables open slowly, about alpha log N: 4.95 expected after 79 customers.
4 One alpha, two lenses (C-04) 5 Gamma(1, 1), J = 100 One prior, two readings: E K_100 = 4.84 clusters, yet Pr(W_SB > .5) = .591.
5 The unintended prior (M-03) 5 the default setting: Gamma(1.60, 1.22), J = 50 A prior tuned to “about five clusters” still puts a random unit in a majority cluster with probability .487.

Researchers: 45 minutes

What default hyperpriors imply, how the published methods fix one summary, and how DPprior calibrates both.

Step Widget Minutes Do Notice
1 Same default, different J (M-01) 5 Gamma(1, 1) across J E K_J = 4.16 at J = 50, 4.84 at 100, 7.82 at 2000, while Pr(W_SB > .5) stays .591.
2 The unintended prior (M-03) 5 default The count target is met; the weight tail (.487) is not stated anywhere.
3 DORO, SCAL and SSI (M-04) 6 J = 500 Each method pins one summary and leaves the other free.
4 The method map (M-15) 6 J = 100, then J = 500 Defaults and methods on one (E K_J, Pr(W_SB > .5)) plane move as J changes.
5 TSMM calibrator (C-02) 6 J = 50, about 5 clusters, variance 10 Two-moment matching gives Gamma(1.4082, 1.0770) in a few Newton steps.
6 Elicitation form (C-05) 5 the STAR preset Three routes to (mu_K, sigma²), and the badge when Pr(W_SB > .5) passes .40.
7 Dual-Anchor frontier (C-08) 6 default The rule picks lambda = .30: Gamma(2.3158, 1.4204), Pr(W_SB > .5) = .398.
8 Partition vs effects (A-11) 6 STAR, then Bangert-Drowns The partition moves (Pr(K_J = 1) .560 to .064 in STAR), the effects do not (RMS .0088).

A course: 90 minutes

The DPM ladder from the bottom rung to the sampler, the exercises, and two bridge pages that turn the ladder into a statement about priors.

Part Widgets Minutes Do Notice
Rung 0 Why nonparametric Bayes? (P-00) 6 K = 2, K = 5, K = infinity Fixed cells against cells that open as data arrive.
Rungs 1-2 Beta and Dirichlet (P-01, P-02) 7 move a from 0.01 to 1000 Small a gives all-or-nothing weights, large a nearly even ones.
Rung 3 Paintbox (P-03, P-04) 7 many components, few clusters Components are the atoms; clusters are the ones units occupy.
Rung 4 Stick-breaking (P-05, P-17) 7 alpha 0.5, 2, 10; the video Size-biased order: large pieces come first.
Rung 5 Hoppe urn (P-06) 5 draw balls The urn and the stick are the same process.
Rung 6 Chinese restaurant (P-07, P-08, P-14, P-15) 12 alpha = 1; 100 replicates K_J is a random variable: E K_100 = 5.19 at alpha = 1.
Rung 7 DP mixture and atoms (P-09 to P-12) 8 generate a DP mixture Components created against clusters that hold points.
Rung 8 Collapsed Gibbs sampler (P-13) 8 one cluster to start The sampler reseats one point at a time.
Exercises Five exercises 15 work them with the widgets Conjugacy, a GEM simulator, how the number of tables grows with N and its expectation by hand, two lenses.
Bridge 1000 restaurants (M-05) 8 Gamma(1.6, 1.22), J = 50 The simulated tables match the exact pmf of K_J (mean 5.05).
Bridge Collapse (M-06) 7 Gamma(1, 1) Prior Pr(K_J = 1) is a statement about the prior, not the data.

Instructor notes

  • Offline. Every widget is a small script bundled with the site, so the pages and the standalone versions also work from a downloaded copy of the site (file://) with no network. The videos and the static fallbacks need no JavaScript at all.
  • The same draws for everyone. Widgets with random draws take a seed (?seed=1 in the links above), so a class sees the same sticks, tables and points.
  • Three confusions to name early. Components against clusters (K_J counts the occupied ones); the size-biased weight W_SB against the largest weight W_max (the first is a lower bound for the second when t ≥ 1/2); prior collapse Pr(K_J = 1) against posterior collapse Pr(K_J = 1 | data). The vocabulary page has the site’s terms.
  • Timing. The course route fits one 90-minute session with the exercises as an in-class break; the beginner route works as the first half hour of a workshop, the researcher route as a lab after a talk on the paper.
  • After the session. The catalog lists all 72 visualizations with what each one shows; the released data page lists the tables the evidence pages read.
