Worked example: J = 50

Catalog C-15 · paper Section 3.5

State a count judgment, calibrate a Gamma hyperprior to it, diagnose the implied cluster sizes, and decide whether to refine.

An analyst expects about five clusters among J = 50 sites, with medium confidence (VIF 2.5, so sigma_K^2 = 2.5 × (5 − 1) = 10). The DPprior workflow runs four steps.

  1. State. mu_K = 5, sigma_K^2 = 10 for K_50.
  2. Calibrate (TSMM). Stage 1 uses a shifted negative-binomial proxy and starts at Gamma(2.667, 2.608), whose implied moments (4.415, 5.618) miss the target. Stage 2 solves the exact moment equations by Newton steps and returns Gamma(1.4082, 1.0770): E K_50 = 5.00, Var 10.00, median 4, 90% interval [1, 11].
  3. Diagnose. The same prior gives Pr(W_SB > .5) = .497: a randomly chosen site has nearly even odds of sitting in a majority cluster. That is above the .40 trigger.
  4. Decide. Dual-Anchor picks the largest weight on the count target (lambda = .30 on a 29-point grid) whose tail is at most .40: Gamma(2.3158, 1.4204), with E K_50 = 5.88, 90% [2, 12] and Pr(W_SB > .5) = .398. A hard bound of .25 is reported separately as a sensitivity analysis: Gamma(5.2745, 2.3059), E K_50 = 7.49, 90% [3, 13].

Turn on Show the Stage 1 initializer to see why Stage 2 is needed.

K_J is the number of fitted components (occupied clusters) among the J units. Pr(W_SB > .5) is a prior probability about the size-biased weight, not about the largest cluster. Exact Gamma parameters come from the replication package; the widget recomputes every number with the site’s engine.
