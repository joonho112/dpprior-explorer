Other defaults: flat, diffuse and objective priors

Catalog M-12, M-13, M-14, M-19

The quasi-degenerate Gamma(eps, eps) expects a single cluster; the K_n-diffuse priors of the 1990s are tied to one n and rule out dominance; the Jeffreys prior contradicts DORO; the Stirling-gamma pins the count and moves the weights.

Four more families of default hyperpriors, each chosen to look uninformative (or stable) in one sense and each informative in another. The Gamma numbers on this page are recomputed in the browser; the Jeffreys and Stirling-gamma priors, which are not Gamma, are integrated in R.

Quasi-degenerate Gamma(eps, eps)

Setting a = b = eps gives a Gamma with mean 1 and variance 1 / eps. As eps shrinks the prior approaches p(alpha) ∝ 1 / alpha and looks flat on the log scale, which is why Gamma(0.001, 0.001) became a reference default (Escobar and West, 1995, let a and b go to 0; BUGS uses Gamma(0.001, 0.001)). The flat stretch runs off toward alpha = 0, where every unit shares one cluster.

  • At eps = 0.037 (the SCAL shortcut at m = 100, rounded) a third of the prior mass lies below alpha = 1e-12 and P(K_100 = 1) = .834: the prior is 83% sure that all 100 units form one cluster.
  • At eps = 0.001, 97% of the mass lies below alpha = 1e-12 and P(K_J = 1) = .992 for J = 50, 100 and 500 alike.
  • The prior that looks least informative about alpha is the most informative about K.

K_n-diffuse priors

In the 1990s the Gamma was chosen by eye so that K_n spread over a wide range at the study’s n: Gamma(5, 0.5) at n = 74 (West and Escobar, 1993) and Gamma(3.5, 0.5) at n = 53 (West, Müller and Escobar, 1994); Vicentini and Jermyn (2025) illustrate the idea with Gamma(10, 1). Drawn on the relative axis k / n, the same prior is wide at one n and narrow at another.

  • Gamma(5, 0.5) at n = 74: E K = 21.0 with a 90% interval of [11, 32], the “wide range” it was chosen for. Gamma(10, 1) covers [5, 9] of 10 units but [15, 34] of 100.
  • The large E(alpha) (7 to 10) that spreads K rules out a dominant cluster: Pr(W_SB > .5) = .013 for Gamma(5, 0.5) and .005 for Gamma(10, 1), against .591 for Gamma(1, 1). Diffuse in K means extreme in the weights.
  • There is no rule behind the choice, so nothing tells you what (a, b) to use at another n.

The Jeffreys prior against DORO

Rodriguez (2013) proposed the Jeffreys prior for alpha: p(alpha) proportional to the square root of the Fisher information of the partition at n units. It depends on n and on nothing else, which makes it “objective” in one sense; DORO’s flat target on the number of clusters is “objective” in another. The two disagree.

  • At n = 100 the Jeffreys prior implies a K_100 that is symmetric about n / 2: P(K = 1) = .054 (all units in one cluster) and P(K = 100) = .054 (every unit alone), five times the flat 1/100, over a nearly flat floor near .007. Its mean, 49.8, is close to the flat target’s, but its ends are not. DORO’s best Gamma, for its part, spikes at 1 and humps near 90.
  • Its tail in alpha falls off like alpha^(−3/2), so it has no finite mean, and 4.3% of the prior mass lies above alpha = 1e4. That mass matters: a grid cut at alpha = 1e4 reports E K_100 = 47.6 instead of 49.8.
  • Neither prior allows much dominance: Pr(W_SB > .5) = .132 for Jeffreys at n = 100 (.302 at n = 10) and .088 for DORO’s fit.

The Stirling-gamma prior against Gamma(2, 4)

Zito, Rigon and Dunson (2024) built a prior on alpha that keeps the expected count where you put it: the Stirling-gamma Sg(a, b, m) has E(K_m) = a / b exactly at its reference size m. With m = n it pins E K_n at every n, where the Gamma(2, 4) they compare with drifts. It is the one existing prior that is count-stable across designs; the paper did not compare it, and its weight implications were not reported.

  • Counts. Sg(4, 1, n) and Sg(1, 0.25, n) keep E K_n = 4 from n = 10 to 2000. Gamma(2, 4) gives 2.86 at n = 50, 3.20 at 100, 4.35 at 1,000 and 5.51 at 10,000.
  • Weights. To hold the count at 4 as n grows, the Sg prior moves alpha toward 0, and the chance that a random unit sits in a majority-mass cluster rises: for Sg(4, 1, n) from .31 at n = 10 to .64 at 100 and .77 at 2000. Gamma(2, 4) keeps .726 at every n. Pinning one lens moves the other.

Prior statements only; K_J (K_n) is the number of fitted components among the J (n) units. The quasi-degenerate and K_n-diffuse read-outs repeat their moments with twice the alpha quadrature nodes; the change is below 1e-13. The Jeffreys prior is not a Gamma: its values and the Stirling-gamma values are integrated in R over the whole alpha line (r-export/06_jeffreys_sg_grid.R, n = 10 to 2000 for Sg). Rodriguez (2013) is cited through Vicentini and Jermyn (2025, Sec. 3.1.4), not from the original. Other sources: Escobar and West (1995); West and Escobar (1993), through Vicentini and Jermyn (2025, footnote 1); West, Müller and Escobar (1994), through Dorazio (2009).
