Part 3 · What default hyperpriors imply

Nine published ways to choose the Gamma hyperprior on alpha, what each implies for the number of clusters and for their sizes, and where each goes wrong.

Every rule for choosing the Gamma(a, b) hyperprior on alpha answers one question and leaves another open. Read through two lenses at once, the count (K_J, the number of fitted components among the J units) and the weights (W_SB, the share of the cluster that a randomly chosen unit belongs to), each published rule says something its users did not ask for. Select a year for its one-line error, the evidence and a link to its explorer.

The story in six steps

  1. The vague default. Gamma(1, 1) is vague about alpha, not about clusters. The expected count drifts with the design (E K_J = 4.16 at J = 50, 6.44 at J = 500) while a random unit’s chance of sitting in a majority-mass cluster stays at .591 for every J. The “conservative” defaults Gamma(1, 3) and Gamma(2, 4) raise that chance to .812 and .726. See Same default, different J and What Gamma(1, 1) actually says.
  2. DORO (Dorazio, 2009) projects onto a flat count target. A Gamma mixture cannot be flat: the closest one is a spike at one cluster plus a hump near n, the gap grows with n, and the prior ends up expecting about n / 2 clusters. See the DORO explorer.
  3. SCAL (Murugiah & Sweeting, 2012) fixes two count tails at every sample size, so the prior probability of a single cluster stays at .34 forever, while the weights are left to chance. Its shortcut a = b = exp(−0.033 m) collapses: at m = 100, P(K = 1) = .834. See the SCAL explorer.
  4. SSI (Vicentini & Jermyn, 2025; the paper’s SSI-Weight) fixes the weights and never mentions J, so the expected count grows without bound: 6.44 clusters at J = 25, 20.9 at J = 5000. See the SSI explorer.
  5. Other defaults. The quasi-degenerate Gamma(eps, eps) looks flat on the log scale and expects a single cluster; the K_n-diffuse priors of the 1990s rule out a dominant cluster; the Jeffreys prior piles mass on one cluster and on all singletons; the Stirling-gamma prior pins E(K_m) but leaves the weights unexamined. See other defaults.
  6. The method map. Placed on one plane (expected count against the majority-mass probability), the rules show that one alpha cannot buy few clusters and little dominance for free. The DPprior Dual-Anchor makes that trade explicit at the design J. See the method map.
