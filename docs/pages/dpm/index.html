Part 1 · Learn the Dirichlet process mixture

A ladder from a two-cell mixture to the collapsed Gibbs sampler, after Broderick’s MLSS 2016 lectures and 2012 tutorial.

Nine rungs, from why a mixture with a fixed number of cells is not enough, through the Beta and Dirichlet distributions, the paintbox, stick-breaking, the urn and the Chinese restaurant process, to the Dirichlet process mixture and a sampler that fits it. Each rung is a page with one or more widgets; click a rung to open it. The badges come from the catalog.

Rung Page Catalog What it shows
0 Why nonparametric Bayes? P-00 A finite mixture fixes K cells; K = ∞ keeps unbroken mass for new cells
1–2 Beta and Dirichlet P-01, P-02 Random weight vectors: small a is all-or-nothing, large a is even; the ternary view at K = 3
3 Paintbox P-03, P-04 Uniform drops on a K = 1000 Dirichlet partition: many components, few clusters, the order-of-appearance staircase
4 Stick-breaking P-05, P-17 How alpha splits a unit stick into weights: E[w_1] = 1/(1 + alpha), Pr(w_1 > .5) = 0.5^alpha
5 Hoppe urn to stick-breaking P-06 Colour shares of Hoppe’s urn converge to a stick-breaking draw; the first colour’s share is Beta(1, alpha)
6 Chinese restaurant process P-07, P-08 Units join clusters in proportion to size, or open a new one in proportion to alpha; how K_N grows
6 EPPF and exchangeability P-14 The seating probability is a product of CRP factors that no arrival order changes
6 K_J as a random variable, and exercises P-15 100 replicated restaurants against the Antoniak pmf; five exercises answered by the engine
7 DP mixture, generated P-09 Lazy stick-breaking: N, components created and clusters grow at different speeds
7 Distributions and DP atoms P-10, P-11, P-12 Beta, Dirichlet, GEM and DP draws under one alpha; a DP draw G and its repeating samples; finite K_N stops at K, the DP’s keeps growing
8 Collapsed Gibbs sampler P-13 Posterior = CRP prior weight × fit, one point at a time; three starts; alpha = 0.01 is a strong prior
NoteAttribution

The ladder follows Tamara Broderick’s lectures Nonparametric Bayesian statistics (Machine Learning Summer School, Cádiz, 2016, parts I–III) and her 2012 tutorial Clusters and features from combinatorial stochastic processes, and borrows their visual conventions: the staircase stick, the piece colours (blue, orange, green, purple, red, yellow), grey for components that exist but hold no data, white for unbroken mass, and a red marker for a uniform draw. Her bnp_tutorial R demonstrations are licensed GPL-3 and are not ported: every widget here re-implements the mathematics in this site’s MIT-licensed engine. Some comparison layouts follow the visual grammar of Ryan Giordano’s presentations. Full list: ATTRIBUTION.md.
